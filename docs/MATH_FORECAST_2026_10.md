The likely path is toward **certified analysis of uncertain signals and graph
models**. Possible useful developments are:

1. Propagate sensor, sampling, kernel and rounding uncertainty to rigorous gap
   enclosures for a precisely defined operator. Existing matrix perturbation
   inequalities are the starting point, not a discovery. The interesting
   question is a tighter, useful and computationally affordable bound for the
   specific measurement-to-graph pipeline.
2. Characterize equivalence classes of signals collapsed by the graph mapping;
   determine minimal extra information needed for reconstruction. The simple
   sign ambiguity is already elementary. Nontrivial constraints, topology and
   uncertainty could lead to useful identifiability theorems.
3. Understand stability versus discrimination in phase/graph filtering, and
   build transformation-respecting alternatives. Graph scattering and graph
   neural network stability already have substantial literature.
4. Only after defining appropriate directed/causal operators: investigate
   which spectral invariants distinguish causal orders. Finite undirected
   Laplacian certificates do not automatically transfer to Lorentzian geometry,
   noncommutative spectral triples or quantum gravity.

The last direction connects to Thomas Gfeller's causal-set program, but a
connection of topics is not a derived physical theory. Low-dimensional
spectral coincidences do not identify a unique geometry, particle, spacetime
dimension or physical interaction.

three falsification controls in `MATH_AGENT.md`. It does not implement the complete proposed denoising
benchmark, generate arbitrary conjectures, train a language model, search for
unexplained physical anomalies or automatically prove new theorems. The fixed
pilot ends at the existing cutoff, not at the forecast horizon.
